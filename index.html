Formal Definition

Definition of a Group (G, ★)

A Group is a non-empty set G equipped with a binary operation ★ satisfying four fundamental axioms:

1. Closure

For all a, b ∈ G, a ★ b also belongs to G.

2. Associativity

For all a, b, c ∈ G, (a ★ b) ★ c = a ★ (b ★ c).

3. Identity Element

There exists e ∈ G such that a ★ e = e ★ a = a for all a ∈ G.

4. Inverse Elements

For every a ∈ G, there exists a⁻¹ ∈ G such that a ★ a⁻¹ = a⁻¹ ★ a = e.

Abelian (Commutative) Group: If a ★ b = b ★ a also holds for all a, b ∈ G, we call (G, ★) an Abelian Group.

✓
Geometric Conclusion
The symmetries of any finite geometric shape form a Group under composition (∘)!

Theoretical Summary & Mathematical Deep Dive

Part 5A • The Big Picture

Unifying Geometry, Algebra & Modular Arithmetic

1. GEOMETRIC SYMMETRIES

Motions Preserving Shape

Reflections and rotations of a polygon form a closed, associative set under composition (∘), with the identity e and every transformation reversible.

2. CAYLEY TABLES & AXIOMS

Abstract Algebraic Structure

Once we record a Cayley table, the 4 Group Axioms (Closure, Associativity, Identity, Inverses) completely govern the system's algebraic behaviour.

3. MODULAR ARITHMETIC (ℤₙ)

Cyclic Clockwork Numbers

Sorting integers into n remainder sectors turns infinite integers into a finite clockwork group (ℤₙ, +ₙ)—mirroring the rotations of a regular n-gon!

Part 5B • Isomorphism Explorer

Same Structure, Different Disguise (G ≅ H)

Two groups are isomorphic if relabelling the elements of one produces the exact same Cayley table colour pattern as the other.

Part 5C • Further Examples & Real-World Applications

Where Group Theory Appears Beyond Polygons

1. PERMUTATIONS & CAYLEY'S THEOREM

Every Finite Group Is a Permutation Group

The symmetric group Sₙ consists of all n! ways to permute n objects. By Cayley's Theorem, every finite group is isomorphic to a subgroup of permutations!

2. CRYSTALS & TOI MĀORI SYMMETRIES

17 Wallpaper Groups & Kōwhaiwhai Friezes

There are exactly 7 frieze groups (1D repeating strips) and 17 wallpaper groups (2D plane patterns). Traditional Māori kōwhaiwhai rafter patterns and tukutuku latticework showcase all 7 frieze symmetry groups through glide reflections, half-turns, and mirror axes.

3. COMBINATORIAL PUZZLES

The Rubik's Cube Group (~4.3 × 10¹⁹)

Each face turn (U, D, L, R, F, B) generates a group of 43,252,003,274,489,856,000 states. Solving sequences use commutators (A B A⁻¹ B⁻¹) to cycle just three cubies at a time!

4. PUBLIC-KEY CRYPTOGRAPHY

Prime Modular Groups (ℤₚ×, ×ₚ)

For a large prime p, non-zero remainders {1, …, p − 1} form a cyclic multiplicative group of order p − 1. Protocols like Diffie–Hellman and RSA rely on modular exponentiation being easy to compute forward yet infeasible to invert.

5. SUBGROUPS & LAGRANGE'S THEOREM

Why Subgroup Sizes Always Divide Group Sizes

Inside the Square group D₄ (order 8), the pure rotations {e, r1, r2, r3} form a subgroup of order 4, and {e, sh} forms a subgroup of order 2. Both 2 and 4 divide 8 evenly! Lagrange's Theorem proves that the order of any subgroup H must divide the order of the finite group G.
